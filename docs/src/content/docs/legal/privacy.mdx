## Scope and contact

Sim Bridge connects devices you pair to a relay you select. This notice describes the app and relay software; a relay operator’s hosting, backups, logs, and legal obligations may add processing beyond that software. Contact **[support@renner.dev](mailto:support@renner.dev)** for app support and privacy questions. If you operate or use a separately hosted relay, also consult that operator’s notice and contact them about data under their control.

## Data used by the app

The Android gateway accesses incoming SMS, phone/call state, and SIM/subscription information when you enable those features and grant the required permissions or roles. A linked client displays decrypted messages and SIM details and can request SMS or call actions on an authorized Android gateway. Message bodies, sender/recipient numbers, verification codes, SIM labels, and command contents are sensitive information.

A locally generated bridge key and device token are stored using platform credential storage. App preferences retain the relay URL, bridge/device identifiers, settings, notification deduplication state, and other session metadata. Android uses a private native queue to persist events and command outcomes for recovery. Decrypted content is available to each paired device holding the key; device access and compromised devices can expose it.

## What reaches the relay

Before message content and commands are uploaded, the app encrypts them with AES-256-GCM. The relay does not receive the bridge root key. Encryption does **not** hide all data: the relay handles device names and identifiers, bridge identifiers, gateway capabilities, enrollment/authentication records, ciphertext sizes, request timing, network addresses, and routing information. Device names are not encrypted; avoid putting sensitive information in them.

The current relay keeps application data only in process memory. It creates no application database or log files and does not read an earlier relay database. Device-token and enrollment-verifier hashes, routing records, short-lived encrypted queues, upload receipts, and optional push subscriptions are volatile. Abuse-protection counters can temporarily include a source IP address; expired counters are removed within approximately two minutes. Authenticated event streams notify devices when work changes; there is no routine two-second network polling.

Memory processing is still processing of personal data. The operating system or hosting provider may write swap, crash dumps, access logs, console output, or diagnostic data independently of the relay. A relay operator must review and control those facilities before promising a deployment has no persistent records. Use HTTPS for internet traffic; encryption of message content does not protect credentials sent over plaintext HTTP.

## Notifications, clipboard, and other recipients

When iOS push is configured, the relay handles the device’s APNs token and submits an encrypted preview to Apple’s push service. Delivery metadata and a generic fallback alert are visible to the push infrastructure; the notification extension decrypts the preview on the paired iPhone. Local OS notifications can show decrypted content to someone viewing that device or its lock screen, depending on OS settings.

Copying a verification code or recovery information puts sensitive data on the OS clipboard. Other apps, clipboard history, and synchronization tools may access it. Automatic code copying defaults off on desktop and can be changed in Settings. macOS cleanup is conditional and its clipboard markers are advisory; Windows and Linux do not guarantee expiry.

Sending an SMS or initiating a call uses the selected Android phone’s mobile carrier. Recipients and carriers receive information necessary to provide ordinary SMS/calling services. End-to-end encryption within Sim Bridge does not turn carrier SMS into an end-to-end encrypted carrier service.

The current first-party app/relay source contains no advertising or marketing analytics integration. Third-party OS, network, hosting, and support services have their own practices. Store privacy labels require a review of the actual distributed dependencies and operating model; “encrypted” does not imply “no data processed.”

## Retention and deletion

Encrypted message buffers, commands, and push previews expire after at most five minutes, with additional count limits. Upload deduplication receipts are bounded and held in memory for at most 24 hours; they must not retain a message payload beyond the message lifetime. Relay enrollment and push subscriptions remain in memory until revoked or the relay process stops. No relay application data survives a restart. The Android gateway keeps durable authorization records, including current device certificates/public keys, identifiers, names, roles, enrollment policy, and an inactive-incarnation record when needed for deletion. These remain on that device until confirmed removal or deletion; its current policy restores pairing after a relay restart. Clients wait for the gateway and keep their existing identities. Clients that are offline beyond the message window can miss messages. Successful upload means relay acceptance, not confirmation that another device displayed or received the message.

Use **Leave bridge** to request removal of that device. Leaving from the authority gateway requests deletion of the entire bridge. Permanent removal needs the Android gateway to save the policy change; local disconnection or an unsuccessful request does not establish permanent revocation. An owner can remove a linked device or use **Delete bridge** in the relay dashboard with that gateway available to save the deletion and disconnect devices. Revocation closes enrollment, but does not erase content already received by another paired device or rotate the shared bridge key. Contact the relay operator about any independent hosting records. Earlier versions wrote databases and logs; upgrading does not erase those earlier files or their backups. Operators must handle them separately and plan identity-preserving migration before replacing a legacy relay. For support correspondence, contact support@renner.dev to request access or deletion, subject to applicable obligations.

## GDPR / DSGVO preparation

GDPR and DSGVO name the same EU regulation. Memory-only relay processing and end-to-end encryption support data minimization and privacy by design; they do not establish legal compliance on their own. Before operating a hosted service, its operator must identify the controller, purposes and lawful bases, relevant recipients/processors, transfer safeguards, actual hosting retention, and how to exercise applicable rights. Those deployment-specific details remain pending here. OS permission grants are not a substitute for that review.

Depending on the applicable legal basis and circumstances, you may have rights to information, access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection, and a complaint to a supervisory authority. Send requests about app support processing to support@renner.dev and requests about a separately operated relay to its operator. The controller must facilitate requests and observe the applicable response deadlines. [EDPB guidance on individuals’ rights](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/respect-individuals-rights_en).

## Your choices

Use only authorized devices, grant only permissions needed for enabled features, choose a relay you trust, keep invitation/recovery data private, and control notification previews and clipboard copying. You may stop the gateway or leave the bridge. The software does not need an advertising identifier or location permission for its current feature set.

Do not include real SMS, phone numbers, recovery codes, live databases, or credentials in public issues. Support requests should use synthetic examples where possible. This notice will be updated when the distributed software or service model changes; review the release-specific notice before enabling a hosted service.
